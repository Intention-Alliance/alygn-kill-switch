## Detailed Analysis by Grant

### ❌ NVIDIA Applied Research Grant — INELIGIBLE

| Aspect | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Eligibility** | ❌ STRICT: "Full-time faculty at accredited academic institution awarding PhD degrees" |
| **Non-academic allowed?** | ❌ No. Explicitly excluded. |
| **Partnership path?** | ⚠️ High risk, complex, time-consuming |
| **Deadline** | March 31, 2026 (8 days) |
| **Verdict** | **SKIP** — Opportunity cost too high |

#### Academic Partnership Risk Analysis

**What would be required:**
1. Identify academic PI willing to serve as applicant
2. Negotiate IP ownership, funding flow, reporting obligations
3. Establish formal subcontract or collaboration agreement
4. Navigate institutional compliance (conflict of interest, overhead, audit)
5. Draft and submit full proposal in 8 days

**Risk factors:**
| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|------|----------|------------|
| IP misalignment | HIGH | Academic institutions typically claim ownership; ALYGN needs to retain Kill Switch IP |
| Funding flow complexity | HIGH | Subcontracts take weeks/months; NVIDIA deadline is 8 days |
| PI commitment uncertainty | MEDIUM | Faculty may not prioritize external project on short notice |
| Compliance audit exposure | MEDIUM | If discovered, could damage future NVIDIA relationships |
| Opportunity cost | HIGH | Time spent here takes away from viable grants (Schmidt, Coefficient) |

**Neutral POV:**
NVIDIA's requirement exists to ensure research quality and academic rigor. While restrictive, it prevents commercial entities from competing for academic research resources. The barrier is legitimate but excludes ALYGN.

**ALYGN POV:**
As an independent governance institution, ALYGN exists precisely because traditional academic structures don't adequately address AI governance gaps. Forcing ourselves into an academic partnership model contradicts our organizational thesis. We should respect NVIDIA's boundaries and find funders aligned with our institutional model.

**Decision:** Do NOT pursue NVIDIA for this cycle. Revisit if they open industry/non-academic tracks in future.

#### Research Agenda Analysis (from PDF)

**What they fund:**
- Basic/applied research on AI safety testing and evaluation
- Benchmark design principles
- Agent sandbox environments
- Automated testing ecosystems
- Contamination-resistant evaluation methods
- Multi-agent emergent behavior analysis

**Explicit exclusions (CRITICAL):**
| Excluded | Included? |
|----------|-----------|
| ❌ Red-teaming | Maybe (automated only) |
| ❌ Socio-technical research (governance, policy, ethics) | NO |
| ❌ Formal verification | NO |
| ❌ Safety improvement methods (fine-tuning, adversarial training) | NO |
| ❌ Catastrophic risk research (CBRN, cybersecurity, self-proliferation) | NO |

**Governance/institutional coordination:** ❌ EXPLICITLY EXCLUDED

#### Kill Switch Alignment Strategy

**Neutral POV:**
Kill Switch is fundamentally a governance/coordination mechanism wrapped in technical infrastructure. Schmidt's exclusions for "socio-technical research" and "governance" are clear disqualifiers if positioned as governance. However, the technical components (hardware attestation, cryptoeconomic incentive design, automated compliance verification) could fit under "automated testing ecosystems" or "evaluation infrastructure."

**ALYGN POV:**
We must reposition Kill Switch from "governance mechanism" to "technical evaluation infrastructure for AI safety compliance."

**Reframing for Schmidt:**
| Current framing | Schmidt-aligned framing |
|-----------------|-------------------------|
| "Hardware-enforced AI safety compliance" | "Hardware-based attestation infrastructure for automated AI safety verification" |
| "Cryptoeconomic incentives" | "Mechanism design for cryptoeconomic safety validation" |
| "Governance and coordination" | "Interoperable evaluation infrastructure for multi-stakeholder safety assurance" |
| "Kill Switch emergency shutdown" | "Certified robustness mechanisms for safety-critical system interruption" |

**Key repositioning:**
- Emphasize **evaluation science** not **governance design**
- Focus on **automated verification** of safety properties
- Frame Bitcoin blockchain as **audit trail infrastructure** not governance layer
- Position as **benchmarking methodology** for hardware-enforced safety

**Proposal angle:**
"Developing obsolescence-resistant evaluation methodologies for hardware-attested AI safety compliance through cryptoeconomic mechanism design"

**Decision:** PURSUE — Strong funding amount, viable timeline, repositionable fit.

## Conclusion

**Key insight:** ALYGN's institutional identity (independent governance organization) is incompatible with NVIDIA's academic-only requirement. Attempting to force-fit through a partnership would:
1. Violate the spirit if not letter of NVIDIA's rules
2. Create IP and compliance risks
3. Distract from genuinely viable opportunities

**Better path:** Embrace our non-academic identity and pursue funders who value independent governance institutions (Schmidt Sciences, Coefficient Giving, SFF).

**Positioning for Schmidt requires careful reframing** — from "governance" to "technical evaluation infrastructure" — but this is achievable and authentic to the technical components of Kill Switch.

**Next milestone:** Schmidt proposal draft by March 30, Coefficient EOI by April 1.
