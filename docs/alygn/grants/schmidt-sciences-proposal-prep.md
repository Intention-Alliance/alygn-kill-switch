## Research Agenda Priorities

The September 2024 Research Agenda identifies five priority areas:

1. **User confidence in generative AI safety**
   - Methods to establish and verify safety properties
   - Transparent risk communication

2. **Obsolescence-resistant safety methods**
   - Techniques that persist across model generations
   - Architecture-agnostic approaches

3. **Broad-applicable testing methodologies**
   - Transferable evaluation frameworks
   - Cross-model safety benchmarks

4. **Scalable benchmarks**
   - Automated safety assessment at scale
   - Continuous evaluation mechanisms

5. **Risk measurement**
   - Quantitative risk modeling
   - Transparent audit trails

## Kill Switch Alignment Analysis

### Priority Area Mapping

| Schmidt Priority | Kill Switch Component | Alignment Strength |
|-----------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Obsolescence-resistant safety methods | Hardware-enforced kill switches (TEE/SGX) | **Very High** — Architecture-agnostic by design |
| Broad-applicable testing methodologies | Cryptoeconomic incentive verification | **High** — Testable safety guarantees |
| Risk measurement | Blockchain-based audit trails | **High** — Transparent, quantifiable metrics |
| Scalable benchmarks | Distributed compliance monitoring | **Medium** — Scales to global deployment |
| User confidence | Public verifiability of safety | **Medium** — External validation |

### Key Alignment Arguments

#### 1. Obsolescence-Resistant Safety Methods

> "Hardware-enforced kill switches operate below the software layer, making them resistant to model-specific changes. A TEE-based enforcement mechanism works equally well for transformer architectures, reinforcement learning systems, or future paradigms yet to emerge."

**Evidence points:**
- TEE technology is model-architecture independent
- Hardware enforcement persists across training runs and deployments
- Secure enclaves provide invariant safety guarantees

#### 2. Broad-Applicable Testing Methodologies

> "Cryptoeconomic incentives create testable safety guarantees. Stake slashing conditions can be empirically verified: if safety violations occur, the economic penalty is automatically enforced. This creates a falsifiable safety mechanism."

**Evidence points:**
- Incentive structures are mathematically verifiable
- Game-theoretic properties can be formally analyzed
- Stake-weighted consensus provides measurable security bounds

#### 3. Risk Measurement

> "Blockchain-based audit trails provide transparent, immutable risk metrics. Every safety-relevant event is logged and publicly verifiable, enabling longitudinal risk assessment and cross-organizational comparison."

**Evidence points:**
- Complete event history on public ledger
- Quantifiable compliance metrics (uptime, violation rates, response times)
- External auditability without privileged access

## Funding Exclusions (Critical)

Schmidt Sciences **explicitly excludes** the following:

| Excluded Category | Why It's Excluded | Kill Switch Implication |
|-------------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Formal verification** | Out of scope | Do not frame as formal methods project |
| **New safety improvement methods** (e.g., fine-tuning) | Excluded focus | Do not propose new training techniques |
| **Pure policy work without technical component** | Insufficient technical depth | Must emphasize hardware/cryptoeconomic engineering |

### Strategic Implications

✅ **Emphasize:** Infrastructure, enforcement mechanisms, measurement frameworks  
❌ **Avoid:** Novel training methods, formal proofs without empirical validation, policy recommendations alone

## Proposed Application Strategy

### Recommended Project Framing

> **"Cryptoeconomic Mechanisms for AI Safety Enforcement: Hardware-Verified Compliance Infrastructure"**

### Positioning Statement

This research develops foundational infrastructure for AI safety enforcement that outlasts specific model architectures. By combining hardware-based attestation with blockchain-verifiable incentives, we create an **empirically testable, continuously auditable safety layer** applicable to any AI system regardless of underlying technology.

### Core Argument Structure

```
1. Problem: Current safety mechanisms are software-dependent and architecture-specific
   → Neither obsolescence-resistant nor broadly applicable

2. Solution: Hardware-enforced compliance with cryptoeconomic incentives
   → Architecture-agnostic (works across models)
   → Empirically testable (falsifiable safety claims)
   → Transparently measurable (blockchain audit trails)

3. Innovation: First system to combine TEE attestation with Bitcoin-based stake slashing
   → Novel integration of hardware security and economic incentives
   → Measurable safety outcomes (not just claimed improvements)

4. Impact: Reusable infrastructure for AI governance
   → Applicable to any model architecture
   → Scales to global deployment
   → Enables external accountability
```

### Methodology Emphasis

| Aspect | Approach | Why It Fits Schmidt Priorities |
|--------|----------|-------------------------------|
| **Empirical validation** | Controlled experiments measuring compliance rates | Risk measurement priority |
| **Benchmark development** | Standardized TEE attestation protocols | Scalable benchmarks priority |
| **Cross-model testing** | Evaluation across diverse AI architectures | Broad-applicable testing priority |
| **Longitudinal analysis** | Multi-month deployment monitoring | Obsolescence-resistance validation |
